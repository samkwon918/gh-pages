# Competitive Moat

A durable, structural advantage that protects a business's profits from competitors — the same way a castle moat keeps invaders out. Coined by Warren Buffett. The difference between a good business and a great one.

## Why Moats Matter

A business without a moat is always one price cut, one competitor, one trend-shift away from losing. A business *with* a moat can raise prices, resist competitors, and survive bad years because customers have strong reasons to stay that go beyond the product itself.

Companies with strong moats are 25% more valuable than those without. More importantly, they're survivable. Moats are what separates businesses that compound from businesses that grind.

## The Five Types of Moat (Morningstar Framework)

**1. Intangible Assets**
Brand, patents, licenses, or regulatory advantages that competitors can't easily replicate.
- *Example:* Apple's brand commands a 30–40% price premium. People pay it not just for hardware but for identity. The brand is the moat.
- *Carity application:* What does the Carity brand stand for that no competitor can easily copy?

**2. Switching Costs**
The pain (financial, emotional, practical) of leaving for a competitor becomes greater than the pain of staying.
- *Example:* Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem — developers would have to rebuild entire technical stacks to switch. The switching cost is infinite.
- *Carity application:* What makes a Carity customer not want to buy from someone else? A subscription? A community? Personalization?

**3. Network Effects**
Each additional user makes the product more valuable for all users.
- *Example:* Amazon Marketplace — more sellers attract more buyers, which attract more sellers.
- *Carity application:* Is there a community, referral loop, or social layer that makes Carity more valuable as more people use it?

**4. Cost Advantages**
Ability to produce at lower cost than competitors — through scale, proprietary processes, or location.
- *Example:* BlackRock's Aladdin — one system serves $20T in assets; the marginal cost of adding another client is near zero.
- *Carity application:* As Carity scales, what unit economics improve? Bulk purchasing? Shipping efficiency? Marketing CAC through word of mouth?

**5. Efficient Scale**
Operating in a market too small or specialized for large competitors to bother entering.
- *Example:* A niche DTC brand serving a hyper-specific community. Amazon won't build a brand for them. That's the moat.
- *Carity application:* What niche does Carity serve that no large competitor will prioritize?

## How to Build a Moat as a Small Business

1. **Brand loyalty first.** Consistently over-deliver. Every interaction is either building or eroding the moat.
2. **Price on value, not competition.** Once you have a moat, comparison shopping becomes irrelevant. 86% of buyers pay more for a great customer experience.
3. **Own the data.** Customer behavior, preferences, repurchase timing — this is proprietary intelligence that compounds.
4. **Create community.** Customers who identify with each other around a brand are the hardest to take away.
5. **Keep raising the bar.** Moats erode if you stop investing in them. The answer to a moat is always: make it deeper.

## The Moat + Flywheel Relationship

Moats protect. Flywheels grow. The best businesses have both:
- The flywheel generates compounding momentum
- The moat makes sure that momentum doesn't leak to competitors
