# Amazon

The greatest e-commerce and cloud infrastructure business ever built — founded on a single obsession (the customer) and a single model (the flywheel) that Bezos ran for 30 years without blinking.

## Core Success Principles

**1. Customer obsession, not competitor obsession.**
Amazon's first leadership principle: "Leaders start with the customer and work backwards." Not: start with what competitors are doing. Not: start with what's profitable. Start with what the customer wants and engineer everything else to deliver it. Bezos left a chair empty in meetings to represent "the most important person in the room — the customer."

**2. The Amazon Flywheel.**
Bezos sketched the flywheel on a napkin in 2001. It works like this:
- Lower prices → more customers
- More customers → more sellers want to list
- More sellers → bigger selection
- Bigger selection → better customer experience
- Better experience → more customers
- More volume → lower cost structure → lower prices again

Each rotation of the wheel makes the next rotation faster. This is the compounding engine. It took years to spin up. Once spinning, almost nothing stops it.

**3. Day 1 mentality.**
Bezos named Amazon's headquarters "Day 1." He moved his office from building to building so it always had that name. Day 1 means: treat the company like it launched today. Urgency. Humility. Customer-focus. Day 2 is stasis, irrelevance, and death. The enemy of Day 1 is bureaucracy, process worship, and the belief that you've figured it out.

**4. Long-term over short-term — always.**
Amazon ran at near-zero profit for years. Bezos reinvested every dollar into growth, infrastructure, and new bets. Shareholders screamed. He didn't care. He was building for decade 3, not quarter 3. AWS, which now prints billions, was considered a distraction when it launched. Amazon Prime was considered financially insane. Both became defining.

**5. Willingness to invent, especially when it's uncomfortable.**
Bezos: "Our competitors are never going to send us a thank you note for launching AWS." Amazon didn't wait for permission to disrupt. It disrupted itself before others could.

**6. Working backwards from the press release.**
Amazon's product development starts with writing the press release for the product — what would we say at launch? If the press release isn't exciting, the product isn't ready. Then you write the FAQ. Then you build. This forces clarity of purpose before a line of code is written.

## The Moat

Amazon's moat is **switching costs + network effects + cost leadership** all at once. Prime members buy more, stay longer, and resist cancellation. Third-party sellers can't leave because Amazon has the customers. AWS has proprietary infrastructure no one can replicate on price. Each moat reinforces the others.

## What Carity Can Learn

- **Build a retention flywheel from day one.** The first sale is not the business. The first sale is just the beginning. What makes a customer buy again? That's where the business lives. Map it out.
- **Work backwards from the customer.** Before building any new product or feature: write the press release. What would you say? Who is it for? Why does it matter to them?
- **Long-term mindset.** CAC (cost to acquire a customer) will always look painful in month 1. The question is lifetime value. Carity should be obsessed with LTV:CAC ratio, not month-1 profit.
- **Never declare Day 2.** The biggest threat to Carity is the moment it stops moving like a startup.
