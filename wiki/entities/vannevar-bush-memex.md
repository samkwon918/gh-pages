# Vannevar Bush — Memex

A 1945 conceptual device described by Vannevar Bush in the essay "As We May Think" — a personal, curated knowledge store with associative trails between documents, widely seen as a prophetic vision of hypertext and personal computing.

## What the Memex Was

Bush envisioned a desk-sized device containing a person's entire library of documents, with the ability to create "associative trails" — links between documents that could be saved, shared, and followed. The key idea: information should be organized by association, the way the human mind works, not by hierarchical index.

The vision was closer to a **personal curated knowledge store** than to what the public web became — private, actively maintained, with the connections between documents as valuable as the documents themselves.

## Connection to LLM Wiki

The [[concepts/llm-wiki-pattern]] is spiritually related to the Memex:
- Private, personally curated collection (not a public index)
- Associative links between documents (the `[[PageName]]` cross-references)
- The connections are as valuable as the content

**The part Bush couldn't solve:** who does the maintenance? Building and maintaining associative trails by hand is prohibitively expensive. Bush imagined humans doing this work manually — in practice, nobody did at scale.

The LLM solves this: it builds and maintains the associative structure automatically, at near-zero cost.
