# Carity

The most important business in this wiki — an organic cotton menstrual pad brand selling high-quality period care to women who've been priced out of it. Carity's mission is not just commerce: it's access.

> *"How is the sky the limit when there are footprints on the moon."*

## The Product

**The Carity Multipack — 50 pads for $19.99**
- 28 regular pads
- 12 large pads
- 10 overnight pads
- Material: 100% Texas-manufactured organic cotton (USDA approved)
- Design: paper-thin profile — dispels the myth that organic = bulky

**What makes it different from every competitor:**
1. **All three sizes in one pack.** No competitor does this. Rael, Honest, Cora, L. — all sell single-size packs. Carity's customer buys one box and is covered for a full cycle.
2. **Organic at a price that works.** $0.40/pad, undercutting Rael's premium single packs — with 50 pads of mixed sizing.
3. **Texas-grown organic cotton.** Local sourcing story + USDA certification = credibility.
4. **Paper-thin.** A genuine product differentiator that contradicts the assumption that organic pads must be thick.

## The Market: Period Poverty Is Real and Getting Worse

This is not a niche. This is a crisis Carity is solving:

- **42%** of US women have experienced period poverty in their lifetime (up from 35% in 2023)
- **1 in 4** students currently struggle to afford period products
- **1 in 3** teens lack access to period products (American Academy of Pediatrics)
- **11.9 million** females affected in the US, including 25% of all adolescents
- **39%** of teens say they can't do their best schoolwork because they lack period products
- **Black and Latina women** disproportionately affected: 23–24% vs. 8% of white women

Carity exists at the intersection of two urgent needs: organic quality (health) and accessible pricing (equity). Most organic brands serve the top of the market. Carity serves everyone else — and everyone else is the majority.

## Target Customers

**Primary: Women who are not financially doing well**
- Managing tight budgets but still deserving of quality, chemical-free period care
- Currently using cheap synthetic pads (Always, Kotex) by necessity, not choice
- Motivated by: health, value, convenience — in that order
- Discovery channels: social media, word of mouth, school programs, community orgs

**Secondary: Middle school girls entering puberty**
- First-time pad users — getting this right for them is a lifecycle win
- This customer, won early, is a potential 40-year relationship
- Parents often make the first purchase; the girl makes subsequent ones
- The school angle: period poverty stats show 1 in 3 teens lack access — Carity can address this through bulk/institutional sales

**The insight:** Carity is price-competitive with other organic brands on a per-pad basis — but offers 3–5× the quantity AND three different sizes in one purchase. The value proposition is massive for a budget-conscious shopper who doesn't want to buy separate packs.

**The gap Rael left open:** Rael targets health-conscious middle/upper-class women. Their branding, website, and influencer marketing signal affluence. Carity targets the woman Rael has never spoken to.

## The Moonshot Vision

There are approximately **65 million women of menstruating age in the US.**
- 42% have experienced period poverty = ~27 million potential core customers
- At $19.99/month subscription × 12 = ~$240/year per customer
- 1% market penetration of the underserved segment = 270,000 customers = **$65M ARR**
- 5% = $324M ARR

This is not a small business. This is a category. The question is not whether the market is big enough — it's whether Carity claims it first.

## Moat-Building Priorities (Specific)

**Moat 1: Brand identity — "Carity is for every woman"**
Every competitor talks to affluent health-conscious consumers. Carity talks to every woman. That positioning, held consistently, becomes a moat. The brand name itself carries the right signal: *carity* sounds like *charity* and *clarity* — care, access, honesty.

**Moat 2: The multipack format**
No one else sells organic mixed-size multipacks at bulk pricing. File a design trademark. Become the name people associate with the format.

**Moat 3: The institutional channel**
Schools, community organizations, food banks, women's shelters — these organizations are desperately looking for affordable organic period products. One institutional account can move hundreds of units. This channel also creates a brand halo: "the brand that my school/shelter trusts."

**Moat 4: Subscription**
The replenishment cycle is perfectly predictable (monthly). Subscription turns a one-time purchase into recurring revenue, lowers CAC permanently, and creates a retention moat. A subscribed customer is worth 3–5× a one-time buyer over 12 months.

**Moat 5: Community**
Women experiencing period poverty don't have a brand that speaks *to* them in this space. A community — even just a supportive email list or social presence — that makes Carity feel like an ally, not just a vendor, is extremely hard for competitors to replicate.
