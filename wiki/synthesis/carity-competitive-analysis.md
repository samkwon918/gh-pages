# Carity Competitive Analysis

A detailed breakdown of where Carity sits in the organic pad market, what every competitor is missing, and the specific gaps Carity can own.

## The Competitive Landscape

### Direct Organic Competitors

**Rael**
- Price: ~$7 for 14 pads = $0.50/pad
- Cotton: Texas organic (same source region as Carity)
- Target: Health-conscious, millennial/Gen Z women, middle-to-upper income
- Marketing: Instagram-heavy, wellness influencers, premium aesthetic
- Miss: Single-size packs only. No budget positioning. No institutional angle.
- *Carity advantage: 50 pads vs 14. Mixed sizing. Same per-pad price. Mission-driven.*

**Honest Company**
- Price: ~$5.95 for 10 pads = $0.60/pad
- Target: Millennial moms, premium grocery/Target shoppers
- Marketing: Jessica Alba brand halo, lifestyle positioning
- Miss: Expensive per pad. Small pack sizes. No accessibility mission.
- *Carity advantage: 40% cheaper per pad. 5× more pads per purchase.*

**Cora**
- Price: $12.89 for 28 pads = $0.46/pad
- Feature: "Buy one, give one" social mission (donates pads to girls in need)
- Target: Socially conscious, higher income women
- Miss: Still premium positioning. No bulk. No multi-size.
- *Carity advantage: Carity IS the brand for women in need — not a brand that donates to them from a distance.*

**Organyc**
- Price: $7.29 for 10 pads = $0.73/pad
- Most expensive per-pad in the organic segment
- Miss: Price point completely inaccessible to Carity's core customer

**L. (by Dollar General)**
- Price: Budget, sold in drugstores
- Organic-ish positioning at accessible prices
- Distribution advantage: physical retail
- *Carity advantage: Superior organic credentials. Online subscription model. Multi-size pack.*

**Always Pure / Infinity (conventional premium)**
- Price: ~$0.22–0.30/pad
- Non-organic. Chemical exposure risk.
- *Carity advantage: Health/organic angle. At $0.40/pad vs. $0.22, Carity is closing the gap — and the organic argument is now easier to make.*

## The Pricing Reality Check

Carity at $0.40/pad is now priced *below* most organic competitors on a per-pad basis (Rael $0.50, Honest $0.60, Cora $0.46, Organyc $0.73). The Prime Days pricing makes the value story even stronger:

- **You get 50 pads** — vs 10–14 from competitors
- **You get all three sizes** — you don't need to buy 3 separate packs
- **One purchase covers a full cycle** — potentially 1–2 months

The message: "You're not paying more per pad. You're paying once, for everything you need."

---

## Carity's Unfair Advantages

**1. The All-in-One Format**
No competitor offers a multi-size organic multipack. This is Carity's product moat. A woman who buys Carity doesn't need to buy regular pads AND overnight pads separately. The format eliminates a shopping decision and a competitor touchpoint.

**2. The Mission-Market Fit**
Carity targets the exact market every premium organic brand has ignored. When you are the *only* brand speaking directly to women experiencing period poverty with an organic product at an accessible price, you have zero true direct competitors.

**3. The Middle School Entry Point**
Getting a girl's first pad brand right is a 40-year customer relationship. Competitors haven't thought about the school/middle school market. Carity is perfectly positioned for it: bulk format, affordable price, organic quality that parents trust, institutional sales potential.

**4. Texas Organic Cotton**
USDA certified organic, American-grown. This is a strong story in a market where "organic" can be vague. "Made with Texas organic cotton" is specific, verifiable, and resonant — especially with customers who care about American manufacturing.

---

## Threats to Watch

**1. Price undercutting from large conventional brands**
Always, Kotex, or Target's in-house brand could launch an "organic-lite" bulk pack at $0.35/pad. Defense: Carity's brand identity and mission, not just price, must become the moat.

**2. Rael entering the bulk format**
Rael has the organic credentials and the distribution. If they launch a 50-pad multipack, they have a Carity-shaped answer. Defense: own the mission narrative first; once Carity = "organic pads for every woman," Rael can't replicate that positioning.

**3. Retail distribution disadvantage**
L. (Dollar General), Always (everywhere) — Carity is DTC online. Women in the deepest period poverty may not be online shoppers. Defense: institutional channel (schools, shelters, food banks) bridges this gap.

## The Positioning Statement Carity Should Own

**For:** Women who deserve organic period care but have been priced out of it
**Carity is:** The only organic cotton pad brand designed for every budget
**Because:** 50 pads, three sizes, one price — organic quality without the organic tax

No competitor can steal this position as long as Carity holds it consistently.

## Market Size Reality Check

- ~65 million women of menstruating age in the US
- 42% have experienced period poverty = ~27 million underserved
- Even 0.5% conversion = 135,000 customers
- At $19.99/order, average 4 orders/year = **$10.8M ARR** from 0.5% penetration
- At 2% = $43.2M ARR
- Subscription model multiplies this significantly

This is not a small market with a charitable mission bolted on. This is a massive, underserved market that Carity is uniquely positioned to lead.
