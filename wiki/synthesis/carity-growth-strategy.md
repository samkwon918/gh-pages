# Carity Growth Strategy

The master strategy document for building Carity into a dominant, category-defining business — updated continuously as more is learned, more sources are ingested, and more sales calls are analyzed.

> *"How is the sky the limit when there are footprints on the moon."*

---

## The Foundational Premise

Carity is not trying to be a good small business. It's trying to build a moat, spin a flywheel, and compound its way to market leadership. Every decision should be evaluated against that ambition — not against what's comfortable, familiar, or safe.

The 7 laws of great companies (see [[synthesis/what-makes-great-companies]]) map onto Carity like this:

---

## Law 1 Applied: Know the Customer Deeper Than They Know Themselves

**The standard:** Amazon leaves a chair for the customer in every meeting. Apple begins product design with the user's emotional experience.

**For Carity:**
- Who is the exact buyer? Not a demographic — a *person*. What is their life like on the day they first hear about Carity? What problem are they experiencing? What have they already tried?
- What do they feel when the product arrives? When they use it? When it runs out?
- What do they tell their friends? (This is the most important question — it defines the natural marketing.)
- What do they *not* know they need that Carity could give them?

**Action:** The sales call analyses (when ingested) will begin answering this. Every call reveals the customer. Every objection is a clue about what they value and fear.

---

## Law 2 Applied: Design the Carity Flywheel

**Draft flywheel:**

```
Obsessive First Experience
(product quality + packaging + delivery speed + surprise)
          ↓
Customer Tells Someone
(word of mouth = the only CAC that compounds)
          ↓
New Customer Acquired at Near-Zero Cost
          ↓
Lower Blended CAC → Better Unit Economics
          ↓
Reinvest in Product Quality + Brand
          ↓
Even Better First Experience
          ↑ (loop)
```

**The second flywheel (retention):**

```
Great Product → Repeat Purchase
          ↓
Subscription or Loyalty Program
          ↓
Predictable Revenue → Bolder Product Investment
          ↓
Better Product → More Repeat Customers
          ↑ (loop)
```

**What spins both flywheels:** the first experience. If it's merely good, neither flywheel starts. If it's genuinely extraordinary — the kind that makes people want to tell someone — both flywheels begin.

**Diagnostic question:** of Carity's last 50 customers, how many referred someone? If the answer is fewer than 10, the first experience needs work before anything else.

---

## Law 3 Applied: Own More of the Stack

**Current unknown:** what layers does Carity currently control?

**Layers to evaluate and own:**
1. **Product design / sourcing** — can Carity have a proprietary product that competitors can't easily copy?
2. **Brand identity** — the name, visual identity, voice, and story that make the product *mean* something
3. **Customer data** — email list, purchase history, behavioral data — the intelligence layer
4. **Post-purchase experience** — what happens after the order ships? Most brands go silent. Silence is a wasted moat-building moment.
5. **Community** — the layer that creates switching costs without contracts

Each of these is a moat. Each one Carity doesn't own is vulnerability.

---

## Law 4 Applied: The Long Game Decisions

**The question:** what can Carity build now — at real cost, with uncertain payoff — that becomes essential in 3–5 years?

Candidates:
- **An email list of 10,000 true believers** (costs: content + time. Payoff: free acquisition channel forever)
- **A subscription model** (costs: operational complexity. Payoff: predictable revenue + moat)
- **A community** (costs: time and care. Payoff: network effect moat)
- **Proprietary data on customer behavior** (costs: systems and discipline. Payoff: product-market fit intelligence no competitor has)
- **A founder brand** (costs: public vulnerability. Payoff: the most trusted form of DTC marketing in 2025)

The question is not "can we afford this." The question is "if we don't build this, what are we building instead — and is that thing worth anything in 5 years?"

---

## Law 5 Applied: Start Building the Moat Now

Carity's moats to begin building immediately, ranked by priority:

**Priority 1: Brand identity moat**
What does Carity *mean*? Not what does it sell — what does it *mean* to the person buying it? The brand moat is built by being absolutely clear about this and expressing it consistently across every touchpoint. This is the Apple lesson.

**Priority 2: Customer data moat**
Every purchase, every email open, every support interaction is data. Collect it, organize it, use it. The brand that knows its customers best wins. Most DTC brands waste this entirely.

**Priority 3: Retention moat**
Boosting retention by 5% increases profits by 25–95% (Harvard Business School data). The mechanisms: subscriptions, loyalty programs, exceptional post-purchase communication, re-engagement campaigns. Building this now, while Carity is small, makes it exponentially cheaper than later.

**Priority 4: Community moat**
A group of customers who feel a shared identity around the brand is the hardest competitive moat to replicate. It can't be bought. It can only be earned. It starts with the first 100 customers.

---

## Law 6 Applied: What Would Disrupt Carity?

The self-disruption exercise: imagine a competitor who is going to take Carity's customers in 3 years. What are they building?

- A better product at the same price?
- A subscription that makes one-time purchase feel obsolete?
- A community that makes the product feel secondary to the belonging?
- A data advantage that lets them personalize at a level Carity can't?

Carity should be building all of these things. Not because competitors are building them now — but because they will be. The time to build the next thing is before the current thing is under threat.

---

## Law 7 Applied: The Daily Learning System

This wiki is the mechanism. Every session:
- New sources ingested → new knowledge added to the flywheel
- New sales calls analyzed → sharper understanding of the customer
- New market research → adaptation to a changing landscape
- New synthesis → smarter decisions

**The competitor who doesn't do this** is operating on 12-month-old assumptions in a market that's changing quarterly. That's a moat in itself.

## Immediate Priorities (First 90 Days)

*To be refined once sales call data is ingested and product details are known.*

1. **Define the customer in one specific human sentence.** Not a persona card. A *person*.
2. **Map the current flywheel.** Draw it. Identify where it leaks.
3. **Audit the first experience.** Order from yourself. What does it feel like?
4. **Set up the data infrastructure.** Email list, purchase tracking, cohort analysis.
5. **Identify the first community.** Who are the 20 most passionate current customers? Get to know them.
